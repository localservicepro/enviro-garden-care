02 — Project Objective

What this engagement achieves

  • Make the site findable in the suburbs A1 actually services. Right now Google has no way of knowing A1 works in Mount Gravatt, Sunnybank or Springwood, because those words do not exist on the site.
  • Build a page for every service instead of one page for all of them. Seven services currently share a single 234-word page. Each one needs to be able to rank on its own.
  • Own the NDIS lawn and yard maintenance niche in South-East Queensland. It is A1's clearest advantage, it carries the highest advertising value of any keyword in this research, and nobody local is targeting it properly.
  • Convert the traffic once it arrives. A business address, a real quote form and structured data are all missing — all three cost bookings.

What this means in plain English

The site is not broken. It loads, it looks reasonable, and the technical hygiene is better than most small trade sites. The problem is narrower and more fixable than that: Google has no idea where A1 works or what specific jobs it does, because the site never says so. It is a brochure that happens to be online, not a set of pages built to be found.

The fix is not a rescue job. It is adding the geography, splitting the services onto their own pages, and telling Google the business exists at a real address in Mount Gravatt.

Structural changes

  • Replace all four H1s with the single H1 above — the other three become H2s
  • Fix the heading order so it runs H1 → H2 → H3 with no skipped levels
  • Work “lawn mowing services Brisbane” into the first paragraph of body copy, naturally, once
  • Add a dedicated Areas We Service section listing every suburb as readable text, grouped by region — Brisbane South, Bayside, Logan, Redlands
  • Add the full business address 1593 Logan Rd, Mount Gravatt QLD 4122 to the header or footer, in text
  • Add a quote form directly on the homepage instead of linking out to /contact-us/
  • Write descriptive alt text on all seven images currently missing it
  • Add a self-referencing canonical tag
  • Deploy LocalBusiness JSON-LD with coordinates, NAP, service types, opening hours and areaServed
  • Add an NDIS trust strip above the fold — registered provider status is the strongest differentiator available and it is currently buried in a logo image

Technical foundations

  • Verify the property in Google Search Console and submit the updated sitemap
  • Install GA4 with call and form-submit conversion events
  • Remove maximum-scale=1.0, user-scalable=0 from the viewport tag so the page can be pinch-zoomed
  • Change the declared language from en-US to en-AU across the site
  • Address the 1,354ms server response — caching, image compression, and trimming unused CSS and JavaScript (77KB and 60KB respectively)
  • Update the footer copyright year

10 — GEO & AEO

Being found by AI assistants

A growing share of “who mows lawns near me” questions never reach a search results page — they get answered directly by ChatGPT, Gemini, Google's AI Overviews or a voice assistant. Those systems read structured data and clearly-worded answers. A1's site currently scores 33/100 on Lighthouse's agentic browsing check, which measures exactly this.

What fixes it

  • Entity clarity — the site must state, in plain readable text, that A1 Lawn Care is a lawn and garden maintenance business at 1593 Logan Rd, Mount Gravatt, servicing a named list of suburbs. An AI cannot infer what a website does not say.
  • NDIS registration stated in text — currently it only exists as a logo image, which no language model can read.
  • FAQ blocks with FAQPage schema on every page — direct question, direct answer, no preamble. This is the format AI answers are extracted from.
  • Consistent NAP across the website, Google Business Profile and every directory listing, so the systems cross-referencing them agree.

FAQ set to deploy

Written as final copy, drawn from the long-tail keyword tier. Each carries a service and a location:

  • How much does lawn mowing cost in Brisbane?
  • Is A1 Lawn Care an NDIS registered provider, and how do I book with my plan?
  • Do you mow lawns in Sunnybank, Carindale and Springwood?
  • How much does palm tree removal cost in Brisbane?
  • When is the best time to top dress a lawn in Brisbane?
  • Do you take away the green waste and clippings after a job?

What this tells us

  • Fox Mowing is sliding. Their traffic has fallen from an estimated 1,023/mo in February 2025 to 354/mo now. The market is not locked up — positions are moving.

14 — Summary

Why this strategy works

  • It fixes a naming problem, not a reputation problem. A1 has been in business since at least 2019, has real reviews and real work photos. The site simply never tells Google where the business operates. That is an unusually cheap problem to solve.
  • The competitive proof already exists in this market. MowBuddy ranks for 342 keywords on a weaker domain than A1's. Fox Mowing ranks for 729. Both did it with suburb pages. This is not a theory — it is the observable pattern on page one right now.
  • Every target is chosen for difficulty, not vanity. The primary keyword sits at difficulty 22 with 590 monthly searches. The head term at difficulty 41 was deliberately passed over. Sixteen suburb terms average under 17. These are winnable positions, not aspirations.
  • The NDIS niche is defensible. It is genuinely hard to copy — a competitor has to actually become a registered provider. A1 already is one and has never said so on its website.
